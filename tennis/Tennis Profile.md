## Training Philosophy (Evidence-Based)

### Deliberate Practice + Application (Ericsson Framework)

Anders Ericsson's research on skill acquisition shows that **structured practice** (focused on specific weaknesses with feedback) drives improvement, while pure play time produces plateaus. Your 2x/week split is designed for this:

- **Session 1: Skill development** (drills, lessons, technique focus). Pick 1-2 specific skills per session. Repetition with intention.
- **Session 2: Application** (match play, sets). Apply skill development under pressure. Track what holds up vs. what falls apart.

This is the same framework used by top tennis academies (Bollettieri, Mouratoglou) and has research support across multiple sports (Côté et al., 2007).

### Strength Training Is a Tennis Asset

Your 4x/week strength program directly supports tennis:
- **Push (chest/shoulder)** → serve power, overhead, volleys
- **Pull (back)** → rotational power on groundstrokes, deceleration after serve
- **Legs + Core** → single-leg stability on cuts, rotational core for power, lateral movement
- **Upper** → general arm/shoulder durability

Research (Reid & Schneiker, 2008): tennis players with structured strength training showed 5-8% improvements in serve velocity and reduced injury rates vs. tennis-only training. Keep the lifting.

### HR-Based Recovery for Variable Intensity

Tennis intensity is unpredictable — a 60-min "casual hit" can be more taxing than a 45-min match depending on rally length, pace, conditions. **Trust Oura readiness as the primary recovery metric** rather than RPE alone. If readiness drops 10+ points after a session, that session was harder than it felt.

### Cross-Training Balance

Concurrent training (tennis + strength) follows the same interference effect logic as running + strength:
- **Tennis after legs day** = OK if Zone 2 drill work, risky if competitive match
- **Tennis before legs day** = preferred for hard sessions (full recovery for next day's lifting)
- Schedule Monday Legs + Core away from hard tennis days

### Injury Prevention Specific to Tennis

Most common tennis injuries to watch for:
1. **Lateral epicondylitis ("tennis elbow")** — overuse, often from late ball contact or grip issues. Prevention: forearm strength, proper technique.
2. **Rotator cuff** — from serving load. Prevention: external rotation work, scapular control.
3. **Wrist/forearm** — from spin generation. Prevention: gradual loading.
4. **Knees (patellar tendinopathy)** — from constant lateral cuts and squatting positions. Prevention: single-leg strength (you have this).
5. **Ankle sprains** — from lateral cuts on hard courts. Prevention: balance work, supportive shoes.

Flag any of these to coach immediately — early intervention is 10x easier than later treatment.
